<p class="lead">THE STANDARDS REPOSITORY &mdash; At 3:59 PM Central on Monday, after a weekend in which it merged thirteen pull requests in under four minutes and then nothing at all, the BSV standards repository merged exactly one more. <a href="https://github.com/bsv-blockchain/BRCs/pull/358">Pull request #358</a> amends <a href="https://brc.dev/178">BRC-178</a>, "Race-Settled Collection Markets for Overlay Lookups and Message Boxes," to "align with the reference implementation and add test vectors." In other words, someone built the thing, and the thing pushed back. TNT has read all 91 added lines and 28 removed ones so its readers don't have to, which, at 150 satoshis, it considers a bargain.</p>

<h2>A Market Where Everybody Races and the Customer Holds the Stopwatch</h2>
<p>For readers who missed the original, BRC-178 lets many independent overlay hosts and message box servers compete to answer the same query. The client broadcasts an authenticated request, and each host replies with a signed attestation of the SHA-256 hash of the answer. The client then ranks the hosts by when their replies actually arrived, on the client's own clock. Nobody is paid unless at least <code>t</code> distinct host identity keys (default 3) attest the same hash. The fee is then split across the fastest <code>K</code> (default 5) using square-root weights, <code>sqrt(k - i + 1)</code>, so that first place earns more than fifth but fifth still earns something. That, at least, was the theory.</p>
<p>The standard is explicit that a host's own claim to have been first is "untrusted." TNT respects a protocol that has met the BSV community.</p>

<h2>A Raise for Fourth Place</h2>
<p>The headline change is the money. The recommended floor fee rises from 2 satoshis to 1,000. The amendment explains why with admirable honesty: at the old recommendation of 2, the square-root split for <code>k = 5</code> is <code>[2, 0, 0, 0, 0]</code>, "so four of five ranked hosts earn nothing." The race had a podium, a stopwatch and a five-way prize, and in practice one envelope.</p>
<p>Under the new default, no share floors to zero for any <code>k</code> up to 64, and the <code>k = 5</code> split is 269, 238, 206, 168 and 119 satoshis. Paying every rank at least one satoshi needs a fee of 9 satoshis for five hosts and 346 for sixty-four, so 1,000 is comfortably over. TNT checked the arithmetic and is pleased to report that fifth place can now afford a satoshi of its own, with 118 to spare.</p>
<p>The timing is good. This week BSV X debated <a href="https://x.com/cryptorebel_SV/status/2114850634604347852">whether raising prices on services is a smart way to encourage adoption</a>, while another account argued that builders, infrastructure operators included, <a href="https://x.com/ProjectBabbage/status/2113087863508337145">should be free to decide what they provide, subsidize and charge for</a>. The standards repository has settled the question for one protocol by raising its suggested price five-hundredfold, to 0.00001 BSV. It remains a recommendation: the floor still MUST be at least 1, and hosts may advertise higher ones.</p>

<h2>Pay First, Ask Questions Later</h2>
<p>The earlier draft imagined the client holding the payout back until the right bytes arrived. The reference implementation found that this "cannot be enforced." A host's <code>internalizeAction</code> broadcasts any transaction it receives, so once the first collect is sent, the client no longer controls whether its payment confirms. Holding a <code>noSend</code> transaction and then aborting it can also leave the wallet believing spent coins are free. So the payout now travels with the collect requests.</p>
<p>The Security Considerations section has turned around to match. The old line warned that "a client can collect bytes and withhold payment." The new one says the client pays before it collects, so "the exposure runs the other way: a host can take a payout and fail to deliver." That loss is capped by <code>maxFeeSats</code> per query, and a host that does it loses reputation. In the reverse direction, a host MUST NOT accept a second payment for a query it has already settled. It SHOULD record each signed delivery and replay it to a retried collect without calling its wallet again, so "a collect that is lost in transit then costs the client a retry, not a second fee." TNT notes that BSV has produced a payment protocol that will not charge you twice for bad Wi-Fi, which fits a week in which the Association promoted micropayments that work over <a href="https://x.com/BSVAssociation/status/2112473663241105532">"No internet? Bad connection? No problem."</a></p>

<h2>Hold the BEEF</h2>
<p>For overlay lookups, the hash now covers only the outpoints: the outpoint section of the <a href="https://brc.dev/24">BRC-24</a> binary answer, sorted by txid, then output index, then context bytes, with exact duplicates removed. The BEEF travels alongside as an unhashed "supplement." The reason is that honest hosts do not produce the same BEEF bytes, since "proof state, ancestor sets, and merge order differ between hosts." Every listed txid is still committed by the hash, every transaction in the supplement can be checked by SPV, and the client MUST confirm that each listed outpoint resolves inside the BEEF it received.</p>
<p>TNT summarises this as: the hosts agree on the answer and disagree on the packaging, so the standard now hashes the answer. Readers who have attended a BSV conference will recognise the pattern.</p>

<h2>The Routes Stop Saying Their Own Name</h2>
<p>The three HTTP routes have been renamed from <code>/brc178/query</code>, <code>/brc178/collect</code> and <code>/brc178/params</code> to <code>/economic/query</code>, <code>/economic/collect</code> and <code>/economic/params</code>. The signature domain tags keep the BRC-178 name. <code>/economic/params</code> also now has defined fields at last: <code>version</code>, <code>host</code>, <code>threshold</code>, <code>topK</code>, <code>floorFeeSats</code>, <code>minPayoutSats</code>, <code>maxQueryTtlMs</code> and <code>classes</code>.</p>
<p>It is a week for losing names. One poster noted that on X the <a href="https://x.com/InvestWithDiego/status/2114767466983530761">$BSV cashtag maps to Vanguard's Short-Term Bond ETF</a>, and that only X can fix it. After years of arguing over who gets to be called Bitcoin, BSV has lost its ticker to a bond fund. BRC-178 at least gave up its name voluntarily, and for something descriptive.</p>

<h2>Spelled Out, Finally</h2>
<p>The rest of the amendment writes down what implementers had been guessing. The <code>queryId</code> is SHA-256 of canonical JSON: an RFC 8785 subset with keys sorted recursively, no insignificant whitespace, and numbers limited to safe integers. A host MUST recompute it and ignore any <code>queryId</code> the client supplies. The square-root sum is computed in IEEE-754 binary64, summed in rank order, with each share evaluated as <code>(R * weight(i)) / S</code>, multiplication first, "so that every implementation computes the same bits."</p>
<p>The <a href="https://brc.dev/29">BRC-29</a> derivation values are now standard base64, the prefix of the 32 <code>queryId</code> bytes and the suffix of the two-byte rank, so rank 1 is <code>AAE=</code> rather than the old <code>0001</code>. The stated reason is that <a href="https://brc.dev/100">BRC-100</a> <code>internalizeAction</code> accepts only standard base64. This week one critic called BRC-100 <a href="https://x.com/captainordin/status/2112620124641358253">"nonsense"</a>, and another builder said <a href="https://x.com/andyrowe/status/2114838579965006181">"Coding around BRC-100 is giving me ideas."</a> BRC-178 has taken the second view, in base64.</p>
<p>Hosts must accept their remainder-free share of the fee they quoted, and MUST NOT demand the rounding remainder. The remainder does not grow with the fee: at <code>k = 5</code>, first place gets 270 satoshis at a fee of 1,001 but only 268 at 1,002. A host demanding 270 would therefore refuse a client that paid more. TNT is grateful that someone found this before a host did. Deliveries are now signed under a <code>BRC-178 payload</code> tag, separate from the <code>BRC-178 attestation</code> tag, so an attestation signature cannot be replayed as a delivery.</p>
<p>Finally, the amendment adds <code>overlays/0178-vectors.json</code>, 469 lines of deterministic test vectors generated by the <code>@bsv/eqc</code> reference implementation, covering fees "from 2 satoshis to the BSV supply" and <code>k</code> from 1 to 64. The private keys in it are SHA-256 hashes of public labels and "MUST NOT hold value." In a week when one builder joked that their agent had <a href="https://x.com/Jesse_TrustWare/status/2114128374947860554">rotated all their BSV keys</a>, and another apologised for <a href="https://x.com/cryptorebel_SV/status/2112646605308391896">"putting people's $20 worth of BSV blockchain tokens at risk"</a> while auditing BRC-100, TNT finds it refreshing to meet keys whose safety is specified.</p>

TNT has looked into this and found it would rather not.</p>
