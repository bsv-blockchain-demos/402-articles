<p class="lead">THE BRC REPOSITORY &mdash; It has been, by BSV standards, a blockbuster month for publicity. A payment sent from a handheld radio with no internet was celebrated across Bitcoin X as <a href="https://x.com/pete_rizzo_/status/2111541505965867446">Bitcoin</a>, to some 337,000 views, until one news account posted an <a href="https://x.com/LightningNewsX/status/2113256491440292221">"IMPORTANT UPDATE"</a>: the video "shows a Bitcoin SV transaction not Bitcoin (BTC). Don't fall for scams." The correction drew more than three times as many replies as the original story. TNT believes this is the first time BSV has been mistaken for Bitcoin by the people who say it isn't, and thanks everyone involved. Back in the paperwork, the BSV standards repository merged twenty-one pull requests in the seven days to Sunday, thirteen of them in under five minutes on the afternoon of the 25th. TNT has read all of them, which makes TNT one of the few parties in the ecosystem able to say so. They cover what a token is, which tokens are more of a token than others, what a wallet should say when it does not know whether your money left, and a proposal under which free blockchain lookups would one day be reserved for addresses beginning "1ChickenNugget." TNT wishes to stress that the last one is not a joke. TNT checked twice.</p>

<h2>The Nugget Schedule</h2>
<p>The proposal was trailed on X on the evening of the 20th by its author's account with the words <a href="https://x.com/ProjectBabbage/status/2112712320518701264">"so thinkin the first million blocks r free then ya gotta pay after that,"</a> followed by "new brc soon i'll explain later 😂". The first reply asked, <a href="https://x.com/Antmantime/status/2112767082559627368">reasonably</a>, "What is brc?" The explanation arrived as <a href="https://brc.dev/234">BRC-234, "A Shared Schedule for Reducing Free Blockchain Index Coverage,"</a> filed in the repository's opinions section. Participating indexers, anchored to block 1,000,000, would gradually shrink the set of transactions, addresses and scripts they promise to look up for free, across 27 increasingly restrictive stages. The first calendar gate falls six months after the anchor and the last 78 months after it; each transition also needs 14 days of chain median time and a cumulative count of non-coinbase transactions that reaches 8,192,000,000 by stage 27. Twetch celebrated <a href="https://x.com/twetchapp/status/2113337633954820125">"1 MILLION HUMAN CLICK TRANSACTIONS ON CHAIN IN LESS THAN 24 HOURS"</a> this week. Stage 27 asks for 8,192 of those days.</p>
<p>The filters are prefixes. At stage 1 a free address must begin with <code>1</code> followed by a character from 6 to H, which the document helpfully notes means an address beginning <code>1K</code> loses its free guarantee at the very first transition. The prefix then narrows through <code>1Ch</code>, <code>1Chic</code> and <code>1Chicken</code> to the final address prefix: <code>1ChickenNugget</code>. TXIDs must meanwhile begin with a growing run of nines, ending at thirteen, and script hashes with sevens, ending at 75 leading bits. The appendix estimates the final address prefix at about 3.3&times;10<sup>22</sup> candidate keys, which TNT believes is the first time a standards document has quoted the price of a nugget in scientific notation.</p>
<p>From stage 13 a transaction must also be version 1 to count, partly because the Chronicle release relaxed malleability rules for higher versions and a ground prefix is no use if the TXID can wander off. Wallets that want the shrinking free ledger to stay accurate would have to make their spends match the prefix too, "usually by grinding transaction candidates." The document says it changes no consensus rule, that adoption is voluntary, that providers may keep full indexes and sell them, and that it "is not evidence that providers have adopted them or that the proposed savings have been measured." It warns that users who keep only a seed "may face costly or unavailable recovery." TNT respects a proposal that lists its own side effects more thoroughly than a pharmaceutical advert.</p>
<p>Reaction was measured, in the BSV sense. One critic called it <a href="https://x.com/cryptorebel_SV/status/2113042476168581348">"central planning"</a>; another, <a href="https://x.com/MoonCoinRising/status/2112871036123259054">"a manipulative attempt at dividing BSV, and forcing a chain split,"</a> which is a lot to ask of a document that changes no consensus rule. A rebuttal paper appeared; the author answered it in a <a href="https://x.com/ProjectBabbage/status/2113087863508337145">single post of nearly 20,000 characters</a>, one point of which ends: "It's in the opinions folder, after all :-)". On the other side of the ledger, one developer asked whether a blockchain is <a href="https://x.com/braydenjlangley/status/2112996796154994772">"some magical database in the sky"</a>, and a wallet team noted that explorers have answered BSV's lookups <a href="https://x.com/HodosBrowser/status/2113255708338888863">"for years with little to no compensation."</a> The stated aim is to move applications toward direct transaction delivery and wallet-held records, rather than an index that everyone uses and nobody pays for. The mechanism is chicken.</p>

<h2>Six Tests, Two Classes, Four Baskets</h2>
<p>Most of Sunday's batch was a seven-document token family. <a href="https://brc.dev/251">BRC-251</a> defines a Bitcoin Native Token (BNT) by six objective tests: the amount is in the locking script, validity follows from transaction bodies alone, only the holder moves it, the rules are fixed at deploy, Bitcoin settles it, and the evidence travels peer to peer. Anything whose issuer co-signs transfers is an Issuer-Permissioned Token (IPT) instead. There is no third option. "Two classes, not a scale," the document says, rejecting a "mostly native" score because it would hide the dependency. TNT notes this is the first token taxonomy in the ecosystem's history with fewer categories than it has acronyms.</p>
<p>The class is decided from the first three bytes of the script, <code>bnt</code> or <code>ipt</code>, before anything else is read. Wallets must file them in separate baskets, <code>bntft</code>, <code>bntnft</code>, <code>iptft</code> and <code>iptnft</code>, must never show an IPT in a BNT balance, and must keep change, consolidation and coin-selection code away from asset outputs entirely. Applications may look at tokens. Only the wallet may touch them. It is, in effect, a museum.</p>
<p>The compact format, <a href="https://brc.dev/255">BRC-255</a>, puts a 3-byte marker and a 20-byte asset id in front of each output and the holder's key inside the lock. A transfer with one input, a payment and change comes to 452 bytes, against 492 for the same transfer as a BRC-162 token with P2PKH. That is 40 bytes, paid again in every later proof, and TNT has seen entire ecosystems founded over less.</p>

<h2>The Proof That Cannot Be Used Yet</h2>
<p>Because a native token's recipient checks every hop back to the deploy, histories get long. <a href="https://brc.dev/252">BRC-252</a> measures the walk at about 4 ms per transaction, so 1,000 transactions take about four seconds and a million take about an hour. Recipients should set a walk limit of at least 1,000 and may refuse anything longer, unless the sender attaches a "step proof": a Groth16 proof that a transaction follows the rules back to its deploy, which the recipient checks itself and nobody has to trust.</p>
<p>Making one is another matter. The network's fans spent the month reminding everyone that Teranode <a href="https://x.com/BullRushClub/status/2111131143915331865">held a million transactions a second for two weeks</a>. Proving a single token transaction on its own took about four minutes, and <a href="https://brc.dev/257">BRC-257</a> adds that it took 19 GB of memory on a laptop. Batching 100 transfers brought that to about five seconds each. BRC-257 therefore creates a proving service, an overlay node that makes the proofs so wallets only have to check them, and that "can refuse or delay but cannot make a false claim count." BRC-252 then adds that the step program's verifying key will be pinned once the program is frozen, and "until then no proof of this type skips a walk." The ecosystem has standardised a shortcut, a vendor for the shortcut, and a sentence confirming that the shortcut is closed. TNT considers this the most honest roadmap it has read this year.</p>

<h2>The Issuer's Pulse</h2>
<p><a href="https://brc.dev/253">BRC-253</a> covers the other class, for assets "whose value comes from their issuer," such as stablecoins, handles and tickets. Every IPT output needs two signatures, the holder's and the issuer's (or <i>m</i> of up to five issuer keys), so a transfer the issuer refuses is simply not valid Bitcoin. The recipient checks an accepted transfer offline from about 0.8 KB.</p>
<p>To prove it is alive, the issuer must keep re-spending a 1-satoshi "beacon" at least once every half of a deploy-set interval. If two beacons land too far apart, the asset has lapsed, permanently, and separate custodians publish a release after which holders move their tokens with their own signature alone. The document puts it plainly: "Nothing in a script can tell an issuer that is gone from one that is refusing you." TNT notes that BSV itself has been <a href="https://x.com/theswansjr/status/2113659434958237872">declared a dead chain</a> on X repeatedly this month, and would welcome a beacon of its own. As one defender asked the regulars, <a href="https://x.com/JohnDoethe33rd/status/2113471343614447647">"If I thought something was dead, I wouldn't bother showing up everyday to tell people."</a></p>

<h2>Selling Things, and Swapping Them</h2>
<p><a href="https://brc.dev/254">BRC-254</a> standardises offers. The seller signs the asset input with <code>SIGHASH_SINGLE | ANYONECANPAY | FORKID</code> (<code>0xC3</code>), which commits to the output paying the seller, so anyone can complete the sale but only by paying that output exactly. It notes that some existing listings use <code>SIGHASH_NONE | ANYONECANPAY</code>, under which "anyone who holds the listing can take the asset and pay nothing." TNT has always believed that a market where the price is optional should at least be documented, and now it has been, in the motivation section of the document that fixes it.</p>
<p><a href="https://brc.dev/256">BRC-256</a> specifies hash-locked swaps between wallets, with the taker's lock standing for 72 hours and the maker's for 48. TNT's coverage policy prevents it from discussing what is on the other side of the swap. It can report that on the BSV side, which "has no time-lock opcode," the refund branch reads the spending transaction's own <code>nLockTime</code> from its signature preimage and proves the preimage genuine, which is either an elegant use of the original protocol or a very long way round, depending on whom you ask and how long they have.</p>

<h2>Also Merged</h2>
<p>Not content with nuggets, Sunday's batch also brought <a href="https://brc.dev/217">BRC-217</a>, which pins the byte order of an authenticated 1Sat inscription and warns that putting the trailing data behind <code>OP_FALSE OP_RETURN</code> instead of a bare <code>OP_RETURN</code> "strands the satoshi forever"; the difference is one byte and does not fail visibly. <a href="https://brc.dev/221">BRC-221</a> requires privacy coin selection to give the same answer however the wallet's data source orders the coins, because otherwise the source gets a say in which of your keys are linked. <a href="https://brc.dev/232">BRC-232</a> delivers unsolicited transactions to a mailbox and reminds everyone that "delivery, wallet acceptance, overlay admission and Bitcoin confirmation are distinct facts." The radio payment proves the point: delivered in about ten seconds, confirmed on chain, and introduced to a third of a million viewers as the wrong network. <a href="https://brc.dev/87">BRC-87</a> now allows digits in service names and raises the length limit from 50 characters to 256 bytes, so names like <code>tm_color_&lt;txid&gt;_&lt;vout&gt;</code> fit. And a cleanup of <a href="https://brc.dev/207">BRC-207</a> and its neighbours removed 547 lines and added 108; BRC-207 now accepts BRC-162 outputs only, while <a href="https://brc.dev/162">BRC-162</a> now requires a 32- or 36-byte id to be a direct, minimal push.</p>
<p>The week's earlier batch, merged on Monday the 19th in about two minutes, was no quieter. <a href="https://brc.dev/326">BRC-326</a> gives wallet failures eight classes, including "uncertain delivery," for a payment that may or may not have happened, which a wallet must not describe as "Failed," "Succeeded," or anything offering a bare retry. <a href="https://brc.dev/325">BRC-325</a> tells apps to explain themselves before Chrome 142's loopback permission dialog appears, since BRC-185 wallet discovery now triggers it, because a site gets that dialog once and a user who declines it rarely goes into site settings to undo it. <a href="https://brc.dev/39">BRC-39</a> caps the Argon2id settings in encrypted wallet exports, having noticed that a file of under a hundred bytes could otherwise ask an importer for almost 4 TiB of memory. <a href="https://brc.dev/175">BRC-175</a> adds "held" payments, acknowledged with <code>"held": true</code>, which a merchant should not treat as paid. <a href="https://brc.dev/215">BRC-215</a> builds block-height deadlines out of OP_PUSH_TX, <a href="https://brc.dev/216">BRC-216</a> specifies a dead-man switch that publishes your sealed document if you stop checking in, <a href="https://brc.dev/222">BRC-222</a> puts multi-party document signing order on chain, and <a href="https://brc.dev/220">BRC-220</a> clarifies which members sit outside its signature.</p>

<h2>The Sentence of the Week</h2>
<p>Read in one sitting, the twenty-one documents share a favourite sentence. BRC-252's transaction host "delays the walk" but cannot substitute a transaction. BRC-256's message host "can delay or drop messages and offers, but it cannot alter, forge or steal anything." BRC-257's prover "can refuse or delay but cannot make a false claim count." BRC-222's coordinator "can delay, and can do nothing else." Even the radio relay's builder described it the same way: it <a href="https://x.com/71Nous/status/2109696148130652216">"cannot change or spend anything, it is only a postbox."</a> The ecosystem has, after years of effort, specified a world in which every intermediary is powerless except to make you wait. TNT notes that this is also an accurate description of the standards process.</p>
<p>Several of Sunday's documents still refer to each other as "Draft BRC-255 (#335)" and so on, having been written while their numbers were, in the words of last week's commits, "proposed numbers; awaiting BSVA assignment." At press time the numbers had been assigned, the documents had been merged, and the cross-references had not been told. TNT expects a follow-up pull request and will cover it in about five minutes. It is aware of the risk. A critic summed up one long BSV reply this week as <a href="https://x.com/theswansjr/status/2113571942372446478">"Five paragraphs and 33 views. BSV really has never been so alive."</a> This column is eighteen paragraphs. TNT looks forward to its readers, both of them, disagreeing about it at length.</p>
