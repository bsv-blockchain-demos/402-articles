<p class="lead">THE STANDARDS REPOSITORY &mdash; Between 12:48 and 12:58 PM Central last Thursday, the BSV standards repository merged fifteen pull requests, roughly one every forty seconds, a pace TNT has previously seen only at buffets. Counting a pair on Tuesday and a pair on Saturday, nineteen pull requests landed after last week's column, adding twelve brand-new BRC documents and amending several old ones. On Monday, a neighbouring account announced <a href="https://x.com/ProjectBabbage/status/2117403408961573040">an interop lab</a> to test whether any of it works together, and separately posted the complete text <a href="https://x.com/ProjectBabbage/status/2117324700313632881">"We need QA"</a>. TNT has read the new documents so its readers don't have to. One of them is 1,206 lines long. It is about a button.</p>

<h2>Now Serving BREAD and BEEF</h2>
<p>The headline item is <a href="https://brc.dev/237">BRC-237</a>, "Payment Evidence Statements for BREAD and BEEF." BEEF readers will know: a bundle that carries a payment's unconfirmed ancestors back to transactions with Merkle paths. BREAD is new to this paper. ElectrumSVP's 0.2.0 release notes, which the BRC quotes, expand it as "Blockchain Referenced Evidence and Data": walk back from the payment through its unconfirmed ancestors until you reach one with a Merkle path to a block header, and count the steps. The BRC observes, fairly, that "three hops" and "a valid BEEF" may describe the same payment, and neither phrase says which checks were actually run.</p>
<p>So a receiver can now write a small canonical JSON statement, version <code>bsv-payment-evidence/v1</code>, recording the anchors it reached, whether every input reaches one (<code>fullyAnchored</code>), the longest and shortest unconfirmed distance to an anchor (<code>distanceMax</code>, <code>distanceMin</code>), and which unconfirmed transactions passed script, amount and lock-time checks. <code>validation</code> is <code>"none"</code>, <code>"partial"</code> or <code>"all"</code>, and the document notes that "a statement whose validation is 'none' conforms." TNT respects a standard that lets you be honest about doing nothing.</p>
<p>The statement's <code>status</code> climbs a ladder from <code>received</code> through <code>signed-locally</code>, <code>handed-to-broadcaster</code> and <code>seen-by-network</code> to <code>mined</code>. A maker MUST say <code>mined</code> only when the payment is itself an anchor, "whatever it was told." And in the abstract, in plain words: "No statement says that a payment will be mined." The week's X timeline argued the same menu from the other side. The Association <a href="https://x.com/BSVAssociation/status/2117272767443665302">picked Section 8 of the white paper</a>, simplified payment verification. One builder noted that headers are cheap and proof of work is easy to check from the header, <a href="https://x.com/ProjectBabbage/status/2117317406318276890">"No object BEEF required."</a> Another asked <a href="https://x.com/cryptorebel_SV/status/2115322767906406446">how many BEEF proofs apps should be sending back and forth every microsecond</a>. The standards repository has answered by adding bread.</p>

<h2>The Button</h2>
<p><a href="https://brc.dev/300">BRC-300</a>, "Sponsored Onboarding and Wallet Choice," governs what a Metanet app shows a person who arrives without a wallet. The control MUST be labelled with connect vocabulary, SHOULD read "Connect wallet," and MUST NOT contain "Sign in," "Log in," "Sign up," "Register" or "Create account." The explanation beside it MUST NOT use "self-custody," "non-custodial," "sovereign" or "decentralized" without saying what they mean, and SHOULD avoid them entirely. After a decade of BSV marketing, TNT considers this the bravest sentence in the corpus.</p>
<p>When no wallet is reachable, a chooser opens, and it MUST present at least two wallets from at least two vendors, because, in the document's words, "a list of one is a product recommendation." Wallet vendors may bid for placement in a sealed-bid, first-price auction decided within 250 milliseconds, and the bid is what the newcomer's wallet gets funded with. The amount is never transmitted, only a commitment to it, so nobody can scrape the offers and sort them. The connect surface MUST NOT use "reward," "bonus," "prize" or "free money." Once the list paints, it MUST NOT reorder until the chooser closes, so nobody installs a wallet they did not choose because a list moved under their finger. Disconnect MUST be reachable in no more steps than connect.</p>
<p>This is a refreshing approach to onboarding for a community whose members report that US banking is <a href="https://x.com/BitcoinSvGuide/status/2117297553788096635">"the most punishing environment imaginable"</a> for them, and complain about filling in every KYC field only to <a href="https://x.com/ProjectBabbage/status/2117325771832504395">learn at the end that their state is blocked</a>. Under BRC-300, wallet vendors bid to fund your new account. The banks, for their part, retain the industry lead in closing one.</p>

<h2>A Hat You May Not See Yet</h2>
<p><a href="https://brc.dev/370">BRC-370</a>, "Sealed 1Sat Ordinal Reveal," inscribes a 1Sat Ordinal's content as ciphertext, AES-256-GCM under a key derived from a <a href="https://brc.dev/369">BRC-369</a> content key, so "the plaintext is never on chain." The sealed field also commits to that key, so one ordinal cannot later open to two different "truths." The owner can then reveal it to everyone with an unspendable <code>OP_FALSE OP_RETURN</code> output tagged <code>TKORV1</code>, or to one recipient with an owner-signed grant that wraps the key to that recipient's identity key with "BIE1" ECIES. On the public reveal, section 3.6 is brief: "There is no retraction." An author section MUST NOT be attached to a sealed ordinal, since it would publish the owner's identity in cleartext beside the secret.</p>
<p>The timing suits CoinGeek, which this week offered <a href="https://x.com/RealCoinGeek/status/2114993340693241955">embroidered snapback caps with digital claims via 1Sat Ordinals</a>. The industry now has the technical basis for a mystery-box hat.</p>

<h2>Log In With Your Email, Eventually</h2>
<p><a href="https://brc.dev/250">BRC-250</a>, "Device Login and Recovery," lets a user sign in to a self-custodial wallet on a new device with an email address: the new device proves the email in zero knowledge, a signed-in device checks the proof, the user types an 8-digit comparison code, and the old device sends the vault encrypted to the new one. The host "never holds the vault or a share of it." The status line says "Draft (proposed number, not a BSVA assignment)." The implementation status section says the v1 prototype is retired, the host now answers <code>410</code> with <code>google-only-recovery-retired</code>, and "the zero-knowledge mail and OAuth proofs below are not implemented." Recovery with only the email is marked withdrawn, because whoever holds the inbox would also hold the proof. If no device has been online in seven days, the request simply waits. TNT admires a standard that ships with its own post-mortem attached, and notes that it is, in that sense, the most interoperable document of the week.</p>

<h2>One Bit per Feature</h2>
<p><a href="https://brc.dev/212">BRC-212</a>, "Compact Wallet Feature Flags in <code>getVersion</code>," packs boolean capability advertisements into one <a href="https://brc.dev/205">BRC-205</a> attribute, six flags per base64url character. A wallet with all four of today's flags returns <code>example-1.2.3; brc212=P;</code>, 24 bytes, instead of a 90-byte string. The motivation is that deployed validators still enforce the original 30-byte limit of <a href="https://brc.dev/100">BRC-100</a>, so 42 features can fit in 30 bytes with a 13-byte base. Future BRCs claim the next free index in its registry "rather than minting a new attribute key." In the week the repository minted twelve documents and the interop lab began minting <a href="https://x.com/ProjectBabbage/status/2117406502449512773">ITCVE-1</a> and <a href="https://x.com/ProjectBabbage/status/2117406738702127139">ISI-1 through ISI-4</a>, TNT welcomes any document that mints less. The interop lab's fourth initiative <a href="https://x.com/ProjectBabbage/status/2117407351334687110">exercises a ToDo app</a>: fund a wallet, create an encrypted task, reload, rebuild it from wallet state, complete and redeem. TNT's own ToDo list currently reads "read BRC-300." The author line of BRC-212 again shares a suspicious number of letters with this paper's byline. TNT has looked into it and continues to prefer not to.</p>

<h2>Also Filed</h2>
<p><a href="https://brc.dev/181">BRC-181</a>, the autonomous-agent spend policy, now requires a signing surface to show who each destination belongs to, since the allowlist it signs is a list of strings "that no person can recognize." Also new: <a href="https://brc.dev/206">BRC-206</a> (Wallet-Native Rabin Signatures as a BRC-98 Module), <a href="https://brc.dev/213">BRC-213</a> (Native UTXO-Chain Coins in a BRC-100 Wallet), <a href="https://brc.dev/214">BRC-214</a> (Wallet Asset Profiles), <a href="https://brc.dev/235">BRC-235</a> (Paymail Public Profile Lookup), <a href="https://brc.dev/246">BRC-246</a> (Session Upgrade: BRC-33 Messages over WebRTPay), <a href="https://brc.dev/247">BRC-247</a> (Identity-Key Issuer Attestation) and <a href="https://brc.dev/249">BRC-249</a> (Vault Account Holding Across Devices). <a href="https://brc.dev/123">BRC-123</a> now admits digits in scheme ids and registers <a href="https://brc.dev/165">BRC-165</a>'s <code>1sat</code> scheme. BRC-100's certificate struct was aligned with its on-wire bytes, the wallet session consent and Action Label grant UX was clarified, and minimal-length HMAC key encoding was specified consistently. Elsewhere, a critic whose audit of BRC-100 ended in a merged pull request to BSV Desktop announced he is <a href="https://x.com/cryptorebel_SV/status/2116723647612326282">"now officially a BRC-bro"</a>. TNT is preparing the membership card. It will be 24 bytes.</p>

<h2>At Press Time</h2>
<p>At press time the fix for publishing brc.dev short links from BRC files had merged, and <a href="https://brc.dev/87">brc.dev/87</a> finally resolves to BRC-87 instead of <code>https://bsv.brc.devnull</code>. TNT had reported that link broken in two consecutive editions and was briefly concerned about losing a beat. It need not have worried: <a href="https://brc.dev/236">BRC-236</a>, Paymail Public Profile Registration, was merged into the BRC-235 branch rather than the main one, and its short link points to devnull. Meanwhile the Association has renamed Arcade to <a href="https://x.com/BSVAssociation/status/2115103427004899526">BSV Broadcaster</a>, the replacement for ARC, with a November 30 deadline it says <a href="https://x.com/BSVAssociation/status/2117272017577668796">"is real"</a>. And after a reported Binance hack, BSV posters noted that their coins were safe, <a href="https://x.com/JohnDoethe33rd/status/2115946002704134416">because Binance delisted them years ago</a>. TNT's security desk confirms that the safest exchange balance remains the one the exchange refused to hold.</p>
