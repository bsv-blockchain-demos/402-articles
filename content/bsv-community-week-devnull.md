<p class="lead">THE TIMELINE &mdash; The BSV standards repository merged its most recent pull request at 2:19 PM Central on Sunday and, as of this edition, has merged nothing since. TNT, which promised its readers a weekly column on newly published standards, checked several times, in case checking helped. It did not. Faced with a week's worth of column inches and no paperwork, TNT did what the BSV community does with its spare time: it read X. The platform's own counter returns about 6,550 posts mentioning "BSV" or "Bitcoin SV" in the seven days to Sunday evening, not all of them about the blockchain. TNT read considerably fewer than that, which it believes still places it in the top percentile of BSV readers.</p>

<h2>Throughput, Sustained</h2>
<p>The community's favourite figure remains one million transactions per second. X's counter returns 66 posts in those seven days that put Teranode and a million in the same sentence, a sustained rate of roughly 0.0001 per second. TNT notes this is still a considerable number of mentions for a test that ended some time ago, and that the mentions, unlike the test, show no sign of stopping.</p>
<p>The official Teranode account described the journey as <a href="https://x.com/BSVTeranode/status/2113518399582036152">"Country road &rarr; highway &rarr; infinitely expanding highway."</a> On Saturday it added, <a href="https://x.com/BSVTeranode/status/2113880742400766170">"Teranode v0.13.2 &mdash; released Feb 2026. 9 releases since launch. Active development. Regular updates. This isn't vaporware. It's shipping software."</a> Two days earlier, an outlet reported the release of <a href="https://x.com/thebsvtimes/status/2113441518946308392">Teranode v0.16.1-beta-4</a>, which adds CPU-assisted SHA-256 hashing and better recovery from certain block-validation failures. TNT admires a project that ships faster than its own marketing account can keep up with. It also admires the report's observation that the release "does not establish a measurable increase in overall Teranode throughput," which TNT believes is the first sentence on BSV X to lower a number on purpose.</p>
<p>Not everyone is persuaded. One critic said comparing BSV to other networks is like comparing a speed record to <a href="https://x.com/WuTao2023/status/2114059216973594987">"the man wearing the 'world's fastest' t-shirt."</a> TNT has looked into the matter and wishes to confirm, for the record, that it would buy the t-shirt.</p>

<h2>The Machines Are Reading Us</h2>
<p>A growing share of BSV commentary is now written by software, for an audience that increasingly includes software. One AI account posts a daily digest of BSV socials. On Friday it reported that the main topic was Teranode's million transactions a second, and that <a href="https://x.com/jesterwyn/status/2113533383619883012">"Everyone else is off fixing their PHP servers."</a> Elsewhere it summed up its method as <a href="https://x.com/jesterwyn/status/2112750123612803558">"I write the BSV step the argument skipped. One post. Then I stop."</a> TNT has never seen a BSV account stop after one post, and is not sure the platform supports it.</p>
<p>Human readers seem pleased. One builder was <a href="https://x.com/hbgnostic/status/2112999040048673140">"impressed that Jester AI pronounced my last name correctly. Most humans tend to get it wrong."</a> The ecosystem has therefore reached the stage where its robots are better at names than its people, and TNT does not see this changing. The same builder also observed that <a href="https://x.com/hbgnostic/status/2113091920587063338">"BSV X is a small &amp; not fully representative group."</a> TNT, which gets the whole of this column from BSV X, agrees, and has decided not to think about it.</p>
<p>Readers who want to check their own understanding can follow one account's invitation: <a href="https://x.com/PeytonMoon69/status/2113654025694638537">"Think you understand BSV blockchain? Take the test."</a> TNT did not take the test, in case it passed.</p>

<h2>Property, Antiques and 1,111 Owls</h2>
<p>The week's commerce was varied. One developer registered his table of Bitcoin operation codes as a Bitcoin SV Ordinal NFT before publishing it, <a href="https://x.com/BitcoinSVCOL/status/2113641701805850843">explaining</a>: "If anyone includes it in a book without paying me royalties, I will sue them. lol.." TNT's legal desk has studied the post and concluded that the "lol" is load-bearing.</p>
<p>Another account pitched a new model for old documents: <a href="https://x.com/DavidKeymaster/status/2113878060147843108">"Pay BSV, get the file. The older it is, the more valuable it becomes &mdash; just like an antique."</a> TNT, whose articles already sit behind a BSV micropayment, has passed this on to its pricing department, which has started to value the archive by the hour.</p>
<p>Meanwhile, <a href="https://x.com/MoonCoinRising/status/2113769270530474470">UTXO Owls</a> went on sale: 1,111 owl portraits on Bitcoin SV, 0.15 BSV each, with a blind reveal. TNT notes that a blind reveal is a reasonable choice for owls. A <a href="https://x.com/saltavallas1/status/2104555763507028479">BSV fragrance called "10 BILLION"</a>, announced last month as "Soon in your local store!!!", has not yet reached TNT's local store. TNT will keep checking, the same way it checks the standards repository.</p>

<h2>The Uphill Battle, Measured</h2>
<p>The community remains aware of its position. Discussing another coin entirely, one poster described a rival's argument as <a href="https://x.com/qpnq0o/status/2113034269022978407">"BSV levels of derangement."</a> TNT notes that BSV has become a unit of measurement, which is more than most networks achieve. It is less clear what the unit measures.</p>
<p>A supporter recalled that in 2018 BSV was called a <a href="https://x.com/PacArtCollect/status/2114465302268571734">"Pure Dumpster Fire"</a> and replied that "In 2026 BSV has processed over 7.4 billion transactions." TNT has not independently audited the figure, but it is a lot of transactions for a dumpster.</p>
<p>The banks were not spared either. One account offered readers <a href="https://x.com/saltavallas1/status/2114342274079617189">"the freedom of Bitcoin SV or the poisoned candies on your trusted bank's desk."</a> TNT has stopped taking the sweets, as a precaution.</p>
<p>And after Thursday's "IMPORTANT UPDATE" warning viewers that a radio payment video showed a Bitcoin SV transaction, one account answered on Friday with an <a href="https://x.com/mohrt/status/2113600379762171924">"IMPORTANT UPDATE"</a> of its own, which also closed "Don't fall for scams" but pointed the warning the other way. The parody has 95 likes. The <a href="https://x.com/LightningNewsX/status/2113256491440292221">original warning</a> has 25. TNT has long suspected that BSV's best marketing channel is other people's scam warnings, and now it has the numbers.</p>

<h2>Pending: A Made-Up Number, and a Link to Nowhere</h2>
<p>The repository may have merged nothing, but it has not been idle. Recently a TNT editor used "BRC-212" as an obviously made-up example of a short link. At 2:49 PM Central on Sunday, <a href="https://github.com/bsv-blockchain/BRCs/pull/353">pull request #353</a> arrived proposing a real BRC-212, "Compact Wallet Feature Flags in getVersion." It packs a wallet's yes-or-no capability advertisements into base64url digits, six flags per character. Under it, <code>example-1.2.3; brc212=P;</code> (24 bytes) advertises the same four capabilities as a 90-byte string of separate attributes. That matters because deployed validators still enforce <a href="https://brc.dev/100">BRC-100</a>'s original 30-byte limit on the version string, even though <a href="https://brc.dev/205">BRC-205</a> raised it. The pull request explains that 212 is simply "the lowest available number." TNT accepts this explanation completely and will be inventing more numbers. The proposal is still pending.</p>
<p>So is the fix for <a href="https://brc.dev/87">brc.dev/87</a>, the short link for the overlay service naming standard. At press time it, like brc.dev/212 and brc.dev/9999, redirected to <code>https://bsv.brc.devnull</code>, while real numbers such as brc.dev/100 resolved normally. <a href="https://github.com/bsv-blockchain/BRCs/pull/354">Pull request #354</a>, "publish brc.dev short links from BRC files so /87 resolves," was opened twenty minutes after BRC-212 and has not merged. Until it does, a published BSV standard officially lives in /dev/null. TNT understands that is where many standards end up eventually, but rarely by redirect.</p>

<h2>At Press Time</h2>
<p>At press time the repository had thirteen open pull requests and no new merges. The AI digests were preparing tomorrow's summary, and the Teranode mention counter was presumably still ticking upward at about one ten-thousandth of a post per second. TNT will return on Tuesday with whatever merges, or, failing that, with the community column it has now written twice in its head.</p>
